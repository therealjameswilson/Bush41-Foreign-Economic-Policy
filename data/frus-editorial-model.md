# Editorial Model

[Foreign Relations, 1977–1980, Volume III, Foreign Economic Policy](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03)

Use the Carter foreign economic policy volume as an editorial model for explaining U.S. choices about the international economic system. The current emphasis is on financial institutions: their resources, lending rules, governance, and the U.S. decisions that shaped them.

These are working editorial judgments for the Bush compilation. The Carter examples illustrate selection and annotation methods; they are not evidence for events in 1989–1992. The Bush proposals retain one continuous chronology and their own first-page NARA PDF provenance.

Basis: [Preface](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/preface); [Sources](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/sources). Reviewed 2026-09-04.

## Selection principles

### Select a consequential choice

Ask what options were available, who advocated them, what was at stake, and what the President or responsible principal decided. A Scowcroft memorandum merits space when it explains that process.

[Model reference](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/preface)

### Read across the economic agencies

Pursue Treasury, State, USTR, CEA, OMB, and, for monetary issues, Federal Reserve and U.S. executive-director records alongside NSC papers. Compare the responsible agency’s case with Scowcroft’s advice.

[Model reference](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/sources)

### Connect advice to negotiation and disposition

Build a sequence from the originating proposal through interagency disagreement, instructions, and a record of bargaining or disposition. Use annotation to connect supporting papers and public outcomes; identify a missing meeting record or unmarked decision explicitly.

[Model reference](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d233)

### Keep the institutional question in view

Select country-specific material when it explains a wider lending rule, voting precedent, debt strategy, or division of responsibility. Use clearly marked extracts where the rest belongs in a regional volume. Preserve the broader monetary, trade, summit, and development-policy remit.

[Model reference](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/preface)

## Examples from the model volume

### [Document 146: Bonn summit minutes](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d146)

July 16, 1978

The minutes preserve exchanges among leaders about monetary arrangements. The edition marks omissions and routes the energy discussion to the energy volume.

Application to the Bush compilation: For Paris and Houston, pair briefing papers with the actual economic discussions and identify the institutional passages to retain.

### [Document 163: Strauss to Carter: Tokyo Round assessment](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d163)

September 22, 1978

A trade negotiator explains the bargain abroad and the coalition needed for congressional approval at home.

Application to the Bush compilation: Seek equivalent Uruguay Round choices, including agriculture, subsidies, concessions, and domestic authority; summit references alone do not complete the trade record.

### [Document 233: Miller: proposed IMF substitution account](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d233)

February 28, 1980

The paper specifies U.S. exposure, safeguards, and negotiating conditions. Annotation reports that no meeting record was found and follows the proposal to suspension.

Application to the Bush compilation: For IMF quotas and EBRD design, retain financial terms and political authority; distinguish a recommended position from a concluded agreement.

### [Document 258: Bergsten to Blumenthal: IDA V negotiations](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d258)

February 28, 1977

A Treasury negotiating report connects donor commitments to congressional appropriations. Notes supply the earlier instructions and later agreement.

Application to the Bush compilation: For IDA, IMF resources, and the Multilateral Investment Fund, trace pledges, appropriations, other donors’ conditions, and the eventual disposition.

### [Document 262: Christopher to Carter: IFI voting and human rights](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d262)

April 1, 1977

An extract isolates the institutional voting issue from an evening report. Annotation records presidential marginalia and the subsequent legislative result.

Application to the Bush compilation: For China, Brazil, and the Philippines, make the general lending or voting principle explicit and retain omission markers in any proposed extract.

### [Document 273: PRC conclusions on North–South strategy](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d273)

July 27, 1977

The record identifies participants, policy questions, conclusions, and assigned work. Its source note separately establishes presidential approval of the summary.

Application to the Bush compilation: For U.S. loan-review procedures, look for agreed instructions and later adjudication; an administrative meeting arrangement is supporting evidence.

### [Document 304: Blumenthal to Carter: development-bank strategy](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d304)

May 19, 1978

A Treasury decision paper compares IDA, IBRD, and IDB commitments, State’s alternative, and budget costs. Editorial notes state where Carter left recommendations unmarked.

Application to the Bush compilation: Favor papers that connect institutions and explain alternatives, funding, and U.S. influence. Blank approval lines must remain unresolved.

## Next research priorities

### Complete the institutional decision sequences

Find Treasury instructions, executive-director positions, board votes, and signed presidential or principals’ dispositions for the IMF, EBRD, and development-bank episodes already proposed. These are follow-up targets, not additional verified selections.

[Document 233](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d233); [Document 258](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d258); [Document 273](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d273)

### Compare the financial agencies’ own records

Use Scowcroft’s files to locate disagreements, then seek the originating Treasury and State analyses and relevant OMB, CEA, Federal Reserve, or executive-director material. Do not infer that a parallel record exists or is released.

[Document 233](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d233); [Document 304](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d304)

### Give trade and monetary policy their own decision records

Extend the current institution-heavy proposal set with verified papers on Uruguay Round negotiating choices, exchange-rate coordination, and international monetary arrangements. Retain the project’s established cross-volume routing for Canada and Mexico.

[Document 146](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d146); [Document 163](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d163)

### Connect debt and development to system-wide policy

Seek choices over concessional resources, creditor burden-sharing, development-bank mandates, and commodity mechanisms where the Bush record establishes their importance. A counterpart to every Carter topic is not required.

[Document 258](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d258); [Document 304](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v03/d304)
