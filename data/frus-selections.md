# Proposed FRUS Selections

Initial document-level selection pass: 11 memoranda from four official file-unit PDFs, dated March 1989–July 1990 (including one undated attachment). Seven are proposed for document treatment and four for annotation. This is a selective starting set, not a complete review of 1989–1992 or of the Scowcroft collection.

Read the official PDF’s first page for archival provenance, then inspect the selected memorandum and its final page for authorship, date, boundaries, classification, and release evidence. Editorial proposals remain subject to compiler judgment and comparison of controlling copies.

## Paris Economic Summit Preparatory Meeting

Memorandum From Eric Melby of the National Security Council Staff to the President's Assistant for National Security Affairs (Scowcroft)

Washington, March 8, 1989

Source: George H.W. Bush Library, Bush Presidential Records, National Security Council, Timothy E. Deal Files, Subject Files, OA/ID CF00972–002, Paris Economic Summit [July 1989]. Confidential.

Proposed treatment: Document. To Scowcroft.

Why select: Consider for the opening of the Paris summit sequence: records the agenda reaching Scowcroft after the February Sherpa meeting and the NSC assessment of French intentions. Connects monetary coordination, debt, East-West trade, and the environment before the principals set U.S. objectives.

Source-image evidence: The one-page memorandum is at PDF page 24, routed through Timothy E. Deal and stamped “Natl Sec Advisor has seen.” The attached six-page Sherpa report is represented by a withdrawal sheet on page 25; its contents are not reconstructed here.

Editorial review: Compare with any released copy of McCormack’s report. If that fuller account is selected, consider citing this covering assessment in annotation.

## Meeting with the White House Summit Group

Memorandum From the President's Assistant for National Security Affairs (Scowcroft) to President Bush

Washington, June 24, 1989

Source: George H.W. Bush Library, Bush Presidential Records, Brent Scowcroft Collection, Latin American Files, Economic Chronological Files, OA/ID 91139–001, Latin America - Economics (June 1989). Secret.

Proposed treatment: Document. From Scowcroft.

Why select: High-priority proposal. A compact statement of U.S. objectives for the Paris summit: stronger monetary coordination, support for the Brady debt strategy, resistance to competing debt proposals, and an ambitious Uruguay Round. It connects exchange-rate policy, commercial-bank negotiations, and presidential bargaining.

Source-image evidence: The initialed three-page briefing is dated June 24; June 26 is the meeting it prepares. Its location in Latin American Files does not limit its substantive coverage to that region. The opening withdrawal inventory lists it, but the three released pages and declassification stamp are present. Page 12 still represents a withheld one-page biography. Agendas and talking points are separate attachments.

Editorial review: Propose the three-page briefing as one document. Route Mexico-specific material by substance while retaining the multilateral debt strategy in Volume XXX; do not mistake the old withdrawal inventory for the present release state.

Source: George H.W. Bush Library, Bush Presidential Records, Brent Scowcroft Collection, Chronological Files, OA/ID 91149–006, Other (July 1990). Secret.

## Houston Summit: World Bank Lending to China

Memorandum From the President's Assistant for National Security Affairs (Scowcroft) to President Bush

Washington, undated

Source: George H.W. Bush Library, Bush Presidential Records, Brent Scowcroft Collection, Chronological Files, OA/ID 91149–006, Other (July 1990). Confidential.

Proposed treatment: Document. From Scowcroft.

Why select: High-priority proposal for the decision process on China lending. Weighs the weakening G-7 consensus, congressional restrictions, and World Bank plans, then recommends a compromise linking expanded lending to economic reform while preserving U.S. legal constraints.

Source-image evidence: The three-page memorandum is undated and has a typed FROM line. Lundsager’s July 1 covering memorandum (page 5) prepared it for the July 2 briefing. The approval and disapproval lines on page 8 are blank. Page 7 prints “$700 billion,” while the conclusion on page 8 and talking points on page 9 say “$700 million”; preserve and flag this discrepancy in any transcription. The document is released despite its listing on the earlier withdrawal inventory.

Editorial review: Retain an undated dateline and do not claim approval or dispatch. Check the China-volume boundary and any signed or annotated parallel copy before final selection. July 1 is solely the chronology sorting reference supplied by the covering memorandum.

Date basis: Undated document; sorted with the July 1, 1990 covering memorandum. Prepared for a July 2 briefing; that is not an established document date.

## Houston Economic Summit

Memorandum From the President's Assistant for National Security Affairs (Scowcroft) to President Bush

Washington, July 2, 1990

Source: George H.W. Bush Library, Bush Presidential Records, Brent Scowcroft Collection, Chronological Files, OA/ID 91149–006, Other (July 1990). Confidential.

Proposed treatment: Document. From Scowcroft.

Why select: High-priority proposal. Scowcroft links economic leadership to the NATO summit, recommends conditionality for Soviet assistance without a major U.S. cash commitment, and treats agricultural reform in the Uruguay Round as Houston’s central economic opportunity. His discussion connects German unification and COCOM reform with summit bargaining.

Source-image evidence: The dated, initialed memorandum occupies pages 12–14. Its first page refers to the Baker–Brady paper, and the July 1 Melby cover on page 15 identifies the two-paper package. All three pages are released and the terminal marking is Confidential. These recommendations do not by themselves establish the President’s decisions or summit outcomes.

Editorial review: Print as a distinct NSC assessment if space permits, with the Baker–Brady paper as a substantive companion. Compare the President’s briefing-book copy and subsequent summit conversations for annotations and outcome evidence.

Date basis: Date printed on the selected memorandum; Washington is printed in its letterhead.
